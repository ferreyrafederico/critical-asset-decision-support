# Decision Definition

## 1. Decision to be Modeled

The company must determine whether to interrupt the operation of the critical asset during an ongoing production cycle in order to perform preventive maintenance.

This decision is made at a specific point in the operational cycle, with incomplete information regarding the true condition of the critical component.

---

## 2. Decision Characteristics

- Type: Operational with strategic impact
- Nature: Discrete (taken once per production cycle)
- Time horizon: 48 to 96 hours (production cycle duration)
- Available information: Partial and uncertain
- Responsible party: Operations Manager in coordination with Maintenance and Quality

---

## 3. Available Alternatives

### Alternative A1 — Preventive Interruption

Interrupt the current production process to perform preventive maintenance on the critical component.

General implications:
- Immediate operational cost
- Planned delay
- Elimination of failure risk during the current cycle
- Higher operational predictability

---

### Alternative A2 — Continue Operation

Keep the asset running until the production cycle is completed.

General implications:
- No immediate cost
- Production plan maintained
- Risk of failure during the cycle
- Potential total loss of in-process product if failure occurs

---

## 4. Non-Tolerable Risks

These are defined as events whose occurrence is unacceptable regardless of expected economic value.

In this case, non-tolerable risks include:

- Asset failure resulting in severe regulatory non-compliance
- Events affecting personnel safety
- Critical reputational damage caused by massive product loss or strategic supply disruption

If any alternative implies an unacceptable probability of these events, it must be discarded before any quantitative analysis.

---

## 5. Scope of This Phase

At this stage, the following are not evaluated:

- Probabilities
- Detailed costs
- Mathematical models

The objective is to clearly isolate the decision and its possible courses of action before moving to quantitative modeling.
